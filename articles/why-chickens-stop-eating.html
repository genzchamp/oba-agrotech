Why do chickens stop eating?

Reduced feed intake is an observation, not a diagnosis. The useful question is what changed around the flock and what other signs appeared with it.

First check: make sure birds actually have reliable access to clean water and feed before assuming the problem is a disease.

1. Check water availability

Inspect drinkers, water flow, leaks, blockage and cleanliness. A water problem can quickly reduce feeding because birds need adequate water intake for normal feeding and production.

2. Inspect the feed

Look at availability, freshness, smell, texture, storage conditions and recent feed changes. Confirm that birds can reach feeders and that feeding equipment is working normally.

3. Check heat and ventilation

Heat stress and poor air quality can reduce appetite. Check temperature, ventilation, ammonia, litter condition and whether birds are distributing normally through the house.

4. Look for stress or management changes

Recent movement, vaccination, handling, sudden changes in routine, stocking changes or other disturbances may affect flock behaviour. Record what changed and when.

5. Observe the birds beyond feeding

Check activity, droppings, breathing, posture, drinking, body condition and mortality. Reduced appetite accompanied by other abnormal signs deserves more attention than a small isolated change.

6. Review the records

Compare current feed and water intake with previous days. A clear trend is more useful than one day's estimate. Also review recent health events and interventions.

When to escalate

If feed intake falls sharply, mortality increases, birds show severe signs or a contagious problem is suspected, seek qualified veterinary assessment. Avoid selecting medication based only on loss of appetite.

Turn appetite into a farm metric

Tracking feed and water consistently helps you notice changes earlier and connect them with other farm indicators.
