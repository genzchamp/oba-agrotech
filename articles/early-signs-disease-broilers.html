Early signs of disease in broiler chickens

The earlier a flock problem is noticed, the more useful your response can be. Learn what to watch without assuming that one sign equals one disease.

Key principle: look for changes from the flock's normal pattern. A single sign is rarely enough to identify a disease confidently.

1. Changes in activity

Watch for unusual lethargy, reduced movement, birds separating from the flock, unusual clustering or changes in the way birds respond when you enter the house.

2. Changes in feed and water intake

A sudden drop or unusual increase in consumption can be an important early signal. Compare observations with the farm's normal records rather than relying only on memory.

3. Changes in droppings

Notice unusual colour, consistency, frequency or distribution of droppings. Droppings can change for many reasons, so they should be interpreted alongside other flock observations.

4. Breathing and posture

Look for coughing, sneezing, nasal discharge, open-mouth breathing, unusual head position, weakness or changes in posture. Environmental conditions can also produce respiratory stress, so check the house as well.

5. Mortality and uniformity

Record deaths by date and, where practical, look at whether birds are becoming noticeably uneven in size or condition. A change in the pattern matters more than one isolated bird.

6. Check the environment

Review ventilation, temperature, litter, ammonia, stocking density, water availability and recent management changes. Health problems can be driven or worsened by conditions inside the house.

What to do when you notice warning signs

Document the signs, timing, age of the flock, recent interventions and relevant records. If signs are severe, mortality is increasing or a contagious disease is possible, seek qualified veterinary assessment promptly.

Build an early-warning habit

Daily observation becomes much more powerful when it is recorded consistently. The aim is to spot meaningful deviations before they become expensive flock problems.
